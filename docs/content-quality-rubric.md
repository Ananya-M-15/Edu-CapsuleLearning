# EduCaps — Content Quality Rubric & Editorial Standards

**Document Version:** 1.0
**Target:** 100+ Course Expansion Framework
**Scope:** Review Checklist, Pedagogical Standards, Disciplinary Boundaries, and Editorial Quality Controls

---

## 1. Quality Principles

EduCaps is committed to bite-sized learning that values the learner's time and intelligence. Because capsules are concise (2–4 minutes to read and practice), every word must carry pedagogical weight.

Micro-learning must never degenerate into shallow trivia, unsubstantiated summaries, or repetitive AI-generated filler. Every capsule published in the EduCaps catalogue must adhere to the 9-dimensional quality rubric detailed below.

---

## 2. The 9-Dimensional Quality Rubric

Reviewers, authors, and AI generation prompts must evaluate draft capsules against these nine core criteria on a 4-point scale:
- **4 — Exemplary:** Exceeds baseline standards; demonstrates exceptional clarity, elegance, and engagement.
- **3 — Proficient (Publishable Threshold):** Meets all pedagogical and factual standards cleanly.
- **2 — Developing (Revision Required):** Fails one or more standards; needs structural revision before publication.
- **1 — Unacceptable (Rejected):** Contains factual errors, misleading claims, or poor pedagogical framing.

### Criterion 1: Accuracy & Factual Reliability
- **Standard:** Every claim, code snippet, mathematical formula, historical date, and technical explanation must be factually correct and verifiable against primary sources or official documentation.
- **Red Flags:** Pseudo-scientific claims, deprecated programming syntax, incorrect order of operations, and speculative statements presented as settled facts.
- **Publishable Threshold:** Must score **4 or 3**. A score of 1 or 2 is an automatic blocker.

### Criterion 2: Age-Appropriate Language & Persona Adaptation
- **Standard:** Vocabulary and narrative tone must align with the target audience and support dual-mode adaptation.
  - **Standard Mode (Mentor: Anaya):** Professional, encouraging, intellectually respectful, and workplace-relevant. Avoids childish oversimplifications while avoiding unnecessary academic jargon.
  - **Kids Mode (Mentor: Cappy):** Playful, concrete, visually oriented, and encouraging. Abstract concepts are translated into physical, tangible metaphors without introducing conceptual errors.
- **Red Flags:** Condescending language in Standard Mode; overly abstract, dense passive voice in Kids Mode.

### Criterion 3: Bite-Sized Cognitive Load
- **Standard:** Content must strictly respect working memory limitations:
  - **Front Card:** 60–120 words (hook, real-world context, core question).
  - **Back Card:** 120–180 words (mental model, concrete example, core lesson, actionable takeaway).
  - **Reading Time:** Under 3 minutes.
- **Red Flags:** Walls of unformatted text, multiple disparate concepts crammed into a single capsule, lack of bullet points or bolded mental hooks.

### Criterion 4: Scaffolded Progression (Simple to Complex)
- **Standard:** Each capsule must build upon concepts introduced in earlier capsules within the topic. Prerequisites must be honored.
- **Red Flags:** Introducing advanced terms (e.g., "polymorphism" or "eigenvalues") without prior foundational explanation; random jumps in difficulty.

### Criterion 5: Practical Relevance ("The Why")
- **Standard:** Every capsule must immediately answer the learner's unspoken question: *"Why does this matter to me in the real world?"*
- **Red Flags:** Abstract definitions presented in a vacuum without real-world utility; theoretical trivia with no practical application.

### Criterion 6: Originality & Anti-Filler Standard
- **Standard:** Every capsule must present a fresh, meaningful insight. Repetitive phrases (e.g., "In today's fast-paced world...", "It is important to remember...") are strictly prohibited.
- **Red Flags:** Boilerplate introductions, redundant summaries that restate the front card verbatim, identical capsule structures copied across different days.

### Criterion 7: Accessibility & Inclusive Framing
- **Standard:** Content must be readable by screen readers and understandable by diverse global learners.
  - Examples must use diverse, realistic names, global contexts, and gender-balanced scenarios.
  - Visual references must include textual descriptions.
  - Readability target: Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level 8–10 for Standard Mode; Grade 4–6 for Kids Mode.
- **Red Flags:** Culturally isolated idioms ("ballpark figure", "touch base"), gender stereotyping, reliance on sensory-only descriptions.

### Criterion 8: Meaningful Quizzes with Plausible Distractors
- **Standard:** Every capsule must include one multiple-choice quiz question with exactly 4 distinct options (A, B, C, D):
  - **Correct Option:** Unambiguously correct and reinforced by the lesson.
  - **Distractors (3):** Highly plausible options based on common human misconceptions or intuitive errors.
  - **Explanatory Feedback:** Comprehensive explanation detailing *why* the correct answer is right and *why* the distractors are wrong.
  - **Kids Mode Hint:** A supportive, friendly nudge from Cappy that guides thinking without spoiling the answer.
- **Red Flags:** Give-away choices like "All of the above" or "None of the above"; silly, obviously fake distractors; trick questions that test grammar rather than concept mastery.

### Criterion 9: Consistency of Terminology
- **Standard:** Terminology must remain coherent across topics, capsules, and adjacent courses. If a variable is called a "container" in Day 1, it should not arbitrarily be called a "bucket" on Day 2 without bridging context.
- **Red Flags:** Conflicting naming conventions within the same module; using deprecated terms alongside modern equivalents.

## 3. Disciplinary Boundary Guidelines

To protect learners and maintain institutional integrity, content in sensitive or rapidly evolving fields must follow strict ethical boundaries:

### 3.1 Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning
1. **Differentiate Concepts from Tool Hype:** Focus on enduring principles (training vs. inference, loss functions, tokenization, embeddings, bias) rather than fleeting proprietary tools or weekly model releases.
2. **No Anthropomorphic Claims:** AI must never be described as "thinking", "feeling", "sentient", or "conscious". Use accurate computational terms: statistical pattern matching, token probability prediction, optimization.
3. **Acknowledge Limitations:** Explicitly teach model hallucinations, training data biases, compute energy costs, and the absolute necessity of human oversight.

### 3.2 Financial Literacy & Money Management
1. **Mandatory Educational Disclaimer:** Every financial course and capsule must maintain the clear boundary that content is for *general educational literacy only* and does not constitute personalized financial, tax, or investment advice.
2. **No Speculative Schemes:** Content must strictly exclude cryptocurrency speculation, day trading, options gambling, get-rich-quick methods, or penny stock promotion.
3. **Evidence-Based Personal Finance:** Emphasize time-tested, risk-mitigated strategies: emergency funds, low-cost index funds, debt avalanche/snowball methods, and compound interest.

### 3.3 Emotional Health, Wellbeing & Psychology
1. **Mandatory Mental Health Disclaimer:** Personal development and habit psychology courses must never present themselves as psychiatric treatment, clinical therapy, or medical diagnosis.
2. **Empathetic & Trauma-Informed:** Never shame the learner for executive dysfunction, financial mistakes, or screen time struggles. Frame behaviors around human evolutionary psychology, neurobiology, and environmental design.
3. **Emergency Crisis Resources:** Where psychological distress or extreme burnout is discussed, provide neutral standard text directing learners to professional healthcare providers.

### 3.4 Cybersecurity, Digital Privacy & Legal Awareness
1. **Defensive Focus Only:** Cybersecurity lessons must focus exclusively on personal defense, hygiene, and authorized defensive testing. Never provide instructions for unauthorized exploitation, credential theft, or cracking.
2. **No Legal Advice:** Digital rights, privacy regulations (GDPR, CCPA), and copyright principles must be presented as informational summaries, not legal counsel.

## 4. Editorial Integrity & Verification Honesty

1. **Zero Fake Endorsements:** Never label content as "expert-verified", "board-certified", "pedagogically validated", or "endorsed by industry leaders" unless a documented, credentialed human expert has performed a signed review.
2. **Source Attribution:** Where empirical claims or scientific studies are referenced (e.g., "The Attention Economy" or "SuperMemo SM-2"), cite the foundational researcher or institution (e.g., Herbert Simon, Dr. Piotr Wozniak, Daniel Kahneman).
3. **Plagiarism Zero-Tolerance:** All capsule copy must be original prose written specifically for the EduCaps micro-learning format. Never copy text verbatim from external blogs, Wikipedia, or textbook publishers.

## 5. Review & Publication Workflow

Before any new batch of capsules is committed or imported into EduCaps fixtures/database:

```
[Draft Authoring]
       │ (Authors apply 5-stage progression & capsule schema)
       ▼
[Automated Linter / Validator]
       │ (Validates schema, word counts, 4 quiz options, feedback presence)
       ▼
[Editorial Peer Review]
       │ (Evaluates 9-dimensional rubric; confirms disciplinary boundaries)
       ▼
[Score Check: Proficient (3+) across all 9 dimensions]
       │
       ├─► Score < 3: Return to Author with Specific Revision Notes
       │
       └─► Score ≥ 3: Approved for Staging Fixture & Database Ingestion
```
